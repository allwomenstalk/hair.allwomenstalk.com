# Chestnut Red Hair: What It Is and What to Ask For

Chestnut red hair is a deep brown-red: enough warmth to read as red, with a brunette richness that keeps it from looking bright orange. If you want a change from brown without a vivid copper transformation, it is a useful reference to bring to a color consultation. The important part is agreeing on the actual shade, because a color name alone leaves plenty of room for interpretation.

Start with the [shade comparison](#shade-comparison) if your saved pictures mix copper, auburn, and cherry cola. Then use the [salon conversation](#salon-conversation) to explain the result you want, and read the [gloss discussion](#gloss-option) before assuming a subtle change means no commitment. A photograph, your current color, and your maintenance preferences tell a colorist much more than the word “chestnut.”

## What chestnut red looks like

Picture a brunette base with a visible red warmth rather than a brown shade that only looks golden in the sun. The reference can lean softer or more saturated, but the brown depth is what makes this particular red feel grounded. It is a color description, not a standardized formula shared by every salon or box-dye brand.

In [Allure’s winter hair report](https://www.allure.com/story/winter-hair-trends-2026), colorist Renée Valerie Radoiu describes chestnut red as a darker direction for copper. Chase Kusero gives this specific instruction: “Ask your colorist for a true saturated copper, not auburn and not strawberry blonde.”

That wording is worth taking literally as his description of the look, rather than translating it into a different formula. A reader might reasonably call a saved photo auburn while a colorist calls the same family copper. Use the image to settle what the label cannot.

## Who might enjoy this shade?

Kadi Lee highlights olive and deeper complexions in [Marie Claire’s color report](https://www.marieclaire.com/beauty/hair/hair-color-trends-fall-2026/); it also mentions a red gloss for dark brunettes.

You do not need to turn that observation into a rule about who is allowed to wear red. For your own decision, hold the reference near a photograph of yourself in ordinary light. Ask which part attracts you: the overall depth, the warm red reflection, or the glossy styling. Those are different goals, and you may want only one of them.

Bring a picture of your hair as you normally wear it, too. A sleek professional blowout can make almost any color reference feel more polished than your everyday style. You want to like the color on your actual haircut and texture, not only on the model in the photograph.

## What to ask your colorist

You can show Kusero’s quoted wording above, then add your own preferences without presenting them as a color formula. Here is our suggested conversation starter:

I like the brown depth in this picture and the red warmth in this one. I do not want the brighter orange effect in this other reference. Can we talk through what would be realistic on my current color?

Tell the colorist about previous color work, including at-home products and any areas that look different from the rest of your hair. Let them assess the starting point rather than asking them to copy a formula designed for someone else.

Before agreeing to the appointment, ask:

- Which reference is closest to a realistic result on my hair?

- What would this look like indoors and outside?

- What would the first service involve, and what would later appointments involve?

- What would it cost to maintain the result I am choosing?

- If I decide I miss my current brown, what would changing direction involve?

These are planning questions. They do not assume a particular technique, appointment schedule, or result. Write down the answers so you can decide without the pressure of already sitting in the color chair.

## Can you try a gloss instead?

A gloss is a discussion to have with the colorist, not a universal substitute for a color service. Ask whether it can create the effect in your reference from your starting shade, and what sort of visible change you should expect. If your favorite picture looks dramatically lighter than your own hair, flag that difference.

Do not treat “gloss” as another way of saying no color, no upkeep, or no possibility of an unwanted result. Ask what the proposed product does, how it is used, and what the plan would be if you wanted to change it later. Follow the product’s directions if you are considering an at-home option.

For a lower-pressure decision, you could first collect references and book a consultation without committing to the full service that day. There is no prize for choosing a red before you understand the plan.

## Keeping the result you actually wanted

Ask for an aftercare plan specific to the service, rather than assembling one from unrelated red-hair videos. Have the salon write down the product type it recommends, how you are meant to use it, and when it wants you to check in. Avoid booking repeat color appointments from a generic schedule that was never designed for your hair.

Take a simple photograph after the appointment in the light you use most often at home. It gives you a reference for the next consultation and helps you explain whether you want the next result warmer, softer, deeper, or closer to brown. A useful follow-up is specific: “I loved this depth, but I want less visible red around my face.”

## Frequently asked questions

### What is chestnut red hair?

It is a descriptive name for brown-red hair with noticeable depth. Bring a picture because salons and brands do not all use the name in the same way.

### Is chestnut red the same as copper?

The terms can overlap. Compare actual references and explain how bright, dark, and warm you want the result to look rather than relying on either word alone.

### Is it the same as cherry cola?

If you are choosing between references, separate the warm brown-red pictures from those with a red-violet appearance. That visual distinction is more useful than assuming the names are interchangeable.

### What should I say at the salon?

Show your favorite example, one result you want to avoid, and a current photograph of your hair. Discuss feasibility, the service plan, cost, and upkeep before deciding.

### Can I get the effect with a gloss?

Ask a colorist whether the proposed gloss can produce your desired change on your starting color. Do not assume every gloss can deliver every chestnut-red reference.

### How long will the shade last?

There is no single timetable promised here. Ask about the particular product and service you choose and use its instructions for maintenance.
