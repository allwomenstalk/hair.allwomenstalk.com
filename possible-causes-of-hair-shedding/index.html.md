# Why Is My Hair Shedding So Much? Fall Shedding and Other Causes, Explained

More hair in your brush can be unsettling, but it does not automatically mean permanent hair loss. Seasonal changes may contribute to fall shedding; illness, childbirth, stress and diet changes can also play a part. A changing part, bald patches or persistent shedding deserve a dermatologist's assessment rather than a guess based on the calendar.

## Is fall hair shedding real?

There is evidence of a seasonal pattern. In a [study of 823 otherwise healthy women who reported hair loss](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19407435/), researchers reviewed hair-cycle measurements collected over six years. The proportion of hairs in the resting phase was highest in summer, with a smaller spring peak and a low point in late winter.

That was a study of resting-hair proportions in a selected group, not a count of hairs everyone loses in October. It cannot tell you why your own hair is shedding. A [Cleveland Clinic Journal of Medicine review](https://www.ccjm.org/content/93/10/616) explains that resting hairs are released after roughly three months, which helps explain why a summer change can become noticeable later. The evidence does not establish a fixed duration for everyone's seasonal shed.

## How much shedding is normal?

The [American Academy of Dermatology](https://www.aad.org/public/diseases/hair-loss/insider/shedding) describes losing about 50–100 hairs daily as normal. The Cleveland Clinic review gives a broader range of about 50–150. These are general estimates, not a home diagnostic cutoff.

Your brush is also an imperfect measuring instrument. Pay attention to a sustained change from your usual pattern and visible changes in density rather than trying to count every strand.

The AAD distinguishes excessive shedding from conditions that stop hair growing. The Cleveland Clinic review adds that shedding can sometimes reveal underlying hereditary hair loss. The table is a guide to the vocabulary, not a way to diagnose yourself.

## Common triggers worth discussing

The AAD and Cleveland Clinic describe several possibilities:

- **A major emotional stressor.** Shedding may appear months after an upsetting event, rather than during it.

- **Childbirth.** Postpartum changes can lead to temporary shedding.

- **Illness, fever or surgery.** A physical stressor may affect the hair cycle.

- **Rapid weight loss or inadequate nutrition.** Low calorie or protein intake and some deficiencies can be relevant.

- **Thyroid problems or other medical conditions.** These need clinical evaluation.

- **Medication or hormonal changes.** These include some medicines and stopping birth-control pills. Do not stop prescribed medication on your own.

Tight hairstyles and harsh processing can also contribute to hair problems, but that is not evidence that an “organic” shampoo prevents shedding. Nor does this evidence support buying a shower filter as a treatment for hair coming out at the root.

## Will it grow back? Should you take supplements?

Recovery depends on the cause. The AAD says fullness often returns within six to nine months after excessive shedding resolves; the Cleveland Clinic review describes noticeable regrowth over roughly six to twelve months after addressing a trigger. These are broad clinical timelines, not guarantees or a reason to postpone an assessment.

The review finds uncertain benefit from supplements such as biotin, zinc or vitamin D when there is no deficiency. Iron treatment is for an identified need, and excessive vitamin A can itself contribute to shedding. A bottle labelled “hair vitamins” cannot identify what is causing the problem.

## When to see a dermatologist

Arrange an assessment if shedding persists for months, your part is widening, you see patches, or your scalp is painful, red or scaly. Mention recent illness, pregnancy, diet changes and medications. You can bring photographs showing the change, without treating them as a diagnosis.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is it normal to shed more in fall?

Seasonal variation is documented, but fall timing alone does not prove that shedding is harmless or seasonal.

### How many hairs a day is normal?

The AAD gives about 50–100; individual patterns vary, and counts alone cannot diagnose hair loss.

### Is shedding the same as hair loss?

Excess shedding means more hairs are released. Other forms of hair loss affect growth, and both can occur together.

### Will hair grow back after shedding?

Temporary shedding often improves after its trigger resolves. The cause and any overlapping hair-loss condition affect recovery.

### Does biotin stop shedding?

The clinical review does not support assuming benefit without a deficiency. Discuss supplements with your clinician.

### When should I get help?

Persistent shedding, visible thinning, patches or scalp symptoms warrant a dermatologist's assessment.
