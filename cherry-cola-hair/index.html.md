# What Is Cherry Cola Hair? The Dark Red Brunette Shade, Explained

Cherry cola hair is a deep brunette with cool cherry-red or burgundy undertones. It may look mostly brown indoors and glow red in daylight, or read as a richer dark red all over. For many dark-haired people, a colorist can create the effect without bleach. Your starting shade and color history still decide how visible that red will be, so the photo you bring to a salon may call for a different process than the one your friend needed.

The name is appealing, but it is not a precise formula. Before you choose a shade, see [how cherry cola compares with burgundy and auburn](#cherry-cola-comparison), then consider [what your current hair color can realistically do without bleach](#cherry-cola-dark-hair). A cool red reflection on untouched dark brown hair is a different goal from a bright red result over old permanent dye.

That distinction matters whether you book a colorist or open a box at home. We walk through [the lower-commitment and permanent-color choices, including dye safety](#cherry-cola-at-home), then the [care that keeps red from fading](#cherry-cola-fade-care). There is also an [exit plan](#cherry-cola-exit-plan) to think about before you fall in love with the first glossy photo.

## Cherry cola, burgundy, and auburn: what's the difference?

The names overlap. [Allure's colorist interviews](https://www.allure.com/story/cherry-cola-hair-trend-2026) describe cherry cola as a brunette base with cool red tones and treat it as a fresh name for burgundy. Auburn usually leans warmer and more copper. Use photos rather than a label alone when explaining what you want.

## Will it work on dark hair without bleach?

Often, yes. New York colorist Michelle Hong told Allure that the shade works particularly well on darker bases without lightening. But very dark or previously colored hair may need a different approach if you want the red to show clearly. Colorist Elizabeth Hiserodt described lifting some hair with color in certain cases; that is a salon decision, not a reliable at-home formula.

Bring two or three reference photos in different lighting and tell your colorist about permanent dye, glosses, henna, and lightener already on your hair. Ask: “Will this read brown indoors or visibly red? How will it fade on my starting color? What would it take to return to brown?” For more inspiration, see these [red-hair looks](https://hair.allwomenstalk.com/proof-that-red-hair-is-the-ultimate-fall-hair-color-in-pics/).

## Can you get cherry cola hair at home?

A temporary or semipermanent color is a lower-commitment way to test the idea. Permanent box dye is harder to predict: the picture on the package cannot account for your existing pigment or previous color. The colorists interviewed by Allure advise extra caution with permanent dye, especially on hair that has already been colored. A salon consultation is the safer route when you want a specific result or expect to lighten your hair.

**Before any home dye:** Follow the package directions and [FDA hair-dye safety advice](https://www.fda.gov/cosmetics/cosmetic-products/hair-dyes). Patch test **every time**, even with a product you have used before. Wear gloves. Do not dye eyebrows or eyelashes, use dye on an irritated or sunburned scalp, or color within 14 days of bleaching, relaxing, or perming. Stop if you have a reaction and seek medical advice.

## How do you keep the red from fading?

Red pigment needs upkeep. Hiserodt suggests waiting at least two days before the first wash, then washing less often with color-safe shampoo and conditioner. Hong recommends reducing hot-tool use, using heat protection, and asking your colorist whether a carefully chosen color-depositing conditioner or gloss makes sense. The wrong tone can push a cool cherry result too purple or too warm. Plan for a gloss or color check roughly every four to six weeks if you want the shade to stay vivid. Our [hair-color fade guide](https://hair.allwomenstalk.com/ways-to-keep-your-hair-color-from-fading/) has more day-to-day care tips.

## Think about the exit plan, too

Dark red can linger when you want to change shades. Colorist Mauricio Bermudez told Allure that moving away from deep red is not always easy unless you are willing to go darker. Ask about that before committing to permanent color.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is cherry cola hair?

It is dark brunette hair with cool cherry-red or burgundy tones. The red can be a subtle reflection or the main event.

### Do you need bleach for cherry cola hair?

Many dark bases do not. Your desired brightness and color history determine whether a colorist recommends any lifting.

### Can you dye it at home?

You can try a lower-commitment color at home if you follow its directions and patch test every time. A specific permanent result is more predictable with a colorist.

### How do you keep it from fading?

Wash less often, use color-safe products, limit heat, and ask about a matching gloss or depositing conditioner.

### Is cherry cola hard to remove?

Deep red can be stubborn. Discuss the eventual change back to brown or another color before your appointment.
