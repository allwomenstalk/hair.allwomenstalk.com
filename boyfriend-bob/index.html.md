# Boyfriend Bob Haircut: What to Ask For and How to Style It

A boyfriend bob is a relaxed, slightly boxy short cut with a blunt-looking edge and minimal layers, usually falling from the chin toward the collarbone. It has structure without looking overly styled. Think of it as a bob that can move, bend and get a little messy on purpose.

The details make the difference: compare [the different bob shapes](#bob-comparison), take a [salon script](#salon-script) to your appointment, and choose a [quick styling routine](#styling) that works with your own texture. Low effort still means finding a length and shape that cooperate with your hair.

## What makes it a boyfriend bob?

Stylist Franck Izquierdo describes it to [Who What Wear](https://www.whowhatwear.com/beauty/hair/boyfriend-bob-trend) as a boxier, more relaxed take on the classic bob, generally cut near the chin or a little longer, with a blunt edge and minimal layering. Stylist Cody Renegar similarly describes a chin-to-collarbone length with a relaxed finish in the publication's [fall hair guide](https://www.whowhatwear.com/beauty/hair/fall-hair-trends-2026). The shape is deliberate; the finish doesn't need to be immaculate.

## Who can wear it?

Izquierdo points to oval, round and heart-shaped faces and suggests adjusting the length or adding volume for a longer face. These are starting points, not rules. Look at where the ends will sit when your hair is dry, and choose the feature you want them to frame.

Waves can give the shape movement without much work. For fine hair, ask about retaining enough weight at the edge; for thick hair, discuss discreet internal shaping if the ends feel too heavy. Those are general consultation ideas, not guarantees. Curls can spring up considerably as they dry, so show your stylist your usual curl pattern and discuss dry length.

## What to ask your stylist

Try: **“I'd like a relaxed bob around my chin or a little longer, with a strong edge and just a little texture at the ends. I don't want lots of visible layers. Can we adapt the length and weight to how my hair dries?”**

Bring two or three reference pictures with a similar texture to yours, including a side or back view if possible. Show your usual part and explain whether you air-dry, use heat, tuck hair behind your ears or need to tie it back. If you're considering fringe, compare it with the softer face framing in the [Elinor haircut](https://hair.allwomenstalk.com/elinor-haircut/).

## A five-minute styling routine

- Blot wet hair gently and part it in the middle or slightly off-center.

- Work a small amount of light styling cream or mousse through the lengths; use less at the roots if your hair is fine.

- Scrunch waves or encourage your natural bend with your fingers. Let it air-dry, or rough-dry with a dryer until mostly dry.

- Once dry, loosen the ends with your fingers. A little texture spray is optional; stop before the hair feels coated.

Five minutes is hands-on time, not a promise that every texture will dry in five minutes. Use heat protection if you heat-style.

## How often does it need a trim?

A sharp perimeter becomes softer as it grows. Ask your stylist when to return based on your growth, texture and whether you prefer a crisp edge or a more grown-in shape. A shorter bob may require more frequent shaping than a collarbone-length version.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is a boyfriend bob different from a regular bob?

It's one variation: the relaxed finish, minimal visible layering and boxier shape distinguish it from a more polished, geometric bob.

### Can fine or thick hair wear one?

Yes, with a tailored perimeter and weight distribution. Bring photos and talk through how your own hair behaves.

### Does it need a center part?

No. A center part is one way to style it, but an off-center or natural part works too.
